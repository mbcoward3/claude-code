How the math works
  • Price path. One price per calendar year, stepping each January 1 by your growth assumption. Grant-year prices and any prices you pin in the editor anchor the path; later years grow from the most recent anchor.
  • Vesting. Equal tranches on September 30, starting the chosen number of years after the grant. Tranches round down to whole shares; the final date picks up the remainder.
  • Loan interest. Accrues daily (actual days ÷ 365) from September 30 of the grant year, first payment due December 31 of the following year — matching the disclosure's day count exactly. Interest is due each December 31; unpaid interest capitalizes into principal at the capitalization rate. Principal is a single balloon due June 30 of the year you set (nine years after the grant, per the standard terms).
  • Share exchange. A down payment paid by exchanging vested shares consumes prior vested shares first, then the oldest modeled grant's vested shares, at that year's price; any shortfall is paid in cash. Exchanged shares leave your holdings.
  • Payroll deduct. Spreads the down payment across the twelve paychecks of the following year. The IRS treats total × loan rate as taxable income that year (not modeled).
  • Walk-away value. Vested shares × current price, plus unvested shares at your purchase price, minus every loan's payoff (principal + interest accrued to the day) — the departure math in the purchase agreement.
  • Not modeled: taxes on gains at sale, imputed-interest tax, tax loans, and minimum-redemption rules.
